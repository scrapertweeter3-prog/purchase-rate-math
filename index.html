DurablePicks

I write buy-it-for-life reviews, where gear gets priced by the decade instead of the receipt: cost per decade, how things fail, what the warranty actually covers. The mug on your desk is the smallest version of that math, so it is a good place to start.

Two mugs, one decade

An $8 camp-style mug usually dies within a year: a chipped handle, a crack down the glaze, a rim you stop trusting. Replaced yearly it costs $80 over ten years and leaves ten mugs behind. A thick-walled ceramic mug around $32, fired and glazed for daily dishwashers, holds for the decade. That is $3.20 a year, under a cent a day. The cheap mug only wins if one survives past year four, which is the break-even, and most never get there.

Where the fine print sits

Lifetime warranties on cheap mugs cover manufacturing defects, not drops and chips, so the promise rarely gets claimed; shipping a $8 mug back costs more than replacing it. The better makers cover real failures for decades, and owners actually file. Reading the coverage before buying tells you which group a mug belongs to. The same reading applies to espresso machines, duffel bags, and work pants: the warranty language predicts the decade better than the price tag does.
